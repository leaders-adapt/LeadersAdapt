# Psychology Principles (v2)

Deploy at least 10 of these 12 in every post, aiming for 8 or more rated Strong. The point is not name-dropping biases; it is structuring the post so a skeptical, busy reader keeps reading and ends up wanting the product.

## The zoning rule (v2, non-negotiable)

All 12 principles live in persuasion zones only: the hook, the narrative body, stories, the conclusion, and the CTA section. Answer zones (Quick Answer, definition callouts, stat blocks, FAQ answers, tables) stay clinical and neutral, because those are the passages AI engines quote, and engines do not quote ads. A persuasion device found inside an answer zone is a scoring deduction, not a credit. The post as a whole still persuades; the quotable blocks stay quotable.

One ethical boundary that is also good marketing: every persuasion element must be true. Real scarcity or none. Real proof or none.

For each principle: what it is, how to deploy, and what earns a Strong (always a quotable line from the post as evidence).

## 1. Zeigarnik Effect
Open loops pull readers forward.
Deploy: tease something specific resolved later, reference the unnamed product component, open a story in the intro and close it near the end.
Strong: 2+ deliberate loops, one spanning most of the post. Weak: none, or loops never closed.

## 2. Peak-End Rule
Readers remember the best moment and the ending.
Deploy: sharpest insight or most vivid scene early; a closing line that could be quoted alone.
Strong: a distinct peak and a landing close. Weak: flat throughout or trailing off.

## 3. Reciprocity
Give real value before asking.
Deploy: a complete usable framework or step sequence the reader can apply today without buying.
Strong: implementable in under an hour from the post alone. Weak: advice that only works if you buy.

## 4. Commitment and Consistency
Small agreements compound.
Deploy: "if you've ever X, you already know Y" constructions, micro-choices, self-identification.
Strong: 2+ small-yes constructions before the CTA. Weak: the only ask is the purchase.

## 5. Social Proof
People follow evidence that others like them moved.
Deploy: real client results and patterns from the evidence bank. Obeys the statistics rule.
Strong: specific, real proof tied to the audience's situation. Weak: "many leaders find" hand-waving.

## 6. Authority
Expertise makes claims land.
Deploy: the author's credential moment from the voice profile, named original frameworks, real citations.
Strong: one first-hand credibility moment plus one named framework or citation. Weak: confident tone, nothing behind it.

## 7. Scarcity
Only honest limits.
Deploy: only if the offer genuinely has limits (cohort size, timing, price change). Mark N/A rather than fabricate.
Strong: a real, specific limit stated plainly. N/A is fully acceptable. Invented urgency is worse than Weak.

## 8. Loss Aversion
Losses weigh twice as much as gains.
Deploy: make the cost of inaction concrete and time-bound; contrast the do-nothing path with the act path.
Strong: a vivid, quantified or time-anchored cost of staying put. Weak: generic "don't miss out".

## 9. Endowment Effect
People value what already feels theirs.
Deploy: ownership language, the reader pictured already using the product.
Strong: ownership framing in body and CTA. Weak: product at arm's length.

## 10. Curiosity Gap
A withheld specific drives action.
Deploy: the one unnamed product component in the CTA section. One gap, conspicuous.
Strong: exactly one engineered gap pointing at the conversion. Weak: none, or clickbait-many.

## 11. Future Pacing
A visualized future gets bought.
Deploy: "picture yourself three months from now" with concrete detail tied to this post's problem, before or inside the CTA.
Strong: a 2+ sentence vivid transformation scene, specific. Weak: "imagine being a better leader".

## 12. Pain-Agitate-Solve
The master structure.
Deploy: open on the pain, agitate with consequences and failed fixes, present the framework and ultimately the product as the systematic version.
Strong: all three beats in order with agitation that feels personal. Weak: solution-first with no stakes.

## Audit honesty

Rate against the Strong criteria, not generously. A post with 6 Strong, 4 Moderate, 1 Weak, 1 N/A is a normal good post. Twelve Strongs in a first draft means the audit was not done seriously. And check the zones: list any principle whose evidence line sits inside an answer zone, and move it.
